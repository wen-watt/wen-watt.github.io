# Read the chain, then decide: what a Solana token is allowed to do, in plain words

## Subtitle: A free, open page lines up WEN and any Solana token from the chain itself. No prices, no rankings, no wallet.

Two tokens can share a name, a cat and a logo and still be very different things. One can be frozen in your wallet by someone else; another takes a fee on every transfer; a third lets a single key move tokens out of any account. None of this shows on a chart. All of it is written in the token, on a public chain, for anyone to read.

So we opened a page that reads it for you: **Compare WEN with any Solana token** (https://wen-watt.github.io/compare-wen.html). Press a button (BONK, JUP, WIF, POPCAT, MEW, USDC, PYUSD, JitoSOL) or paste any mint address, and the page reads both tokens straight from Solana.

## What the page shows

Fourteen properties side by side, each with a **What it means** button:

- **Who can still create tokens.** If the mint authority is revoked, the supply can never grow.
- **Who can freeze you.** A freeze authority can lock a holder's account so it cannot send.
- **A permanent delegate.** A key that can move or burn tokens from any holder, without asking.
- **A transfer fee.** A share taken by the issuer on every transfer, separate from the tiny network fee.
- **A transfer hook.** A program that runs on every transfer; whoever holds its authority can change the rule.
- **Paused, closed, non-transferable, frozen by default.** Rules that change what you can do with the token.
- **Where the name and picture live.** Inside the token, or in a separate account.

Rows that differ are tinted. Keys that can still act are counted. That is all: no score, no "better" or "worse".

## Why no ranking

A key that can act is not good or bad by itself. A regulated stablecoin often must keep the power to freeze; a community memecoin usually gives every power up. What matters is that you know which one you hold, and that you can check it yourself. The page gives the facts and the way to repeat the check; the decision stays with the reader.

## More than the mint

- **Built around WEN, checked live.** WEN began as a poem minted as the first NFT on the Wen New Standard (WNS). The page reads the WNS program and its royalty program, which pays creators through a transfer hook, and says whether both are live code on Solana right now.
- **20 facts from the Wen Almanac record.** What the chain cannot show: the poem behind the supply, the open standard, the airdrop to over a million wallets, the burn, the weekly mint check, the free tools for shops. Each with its source and a status (verified or reported). For the other token the page writes only "No record in the Wen Almanac": we have not recorded it, which says nothing against the token.
- **Is there an encyclopedia page?** The page asks the public IQ.wiki whether a wiki page exists, and calls it found only if the page lists the same mint address (or was checked by hand). If nothing is found, it says so and suggests searching yourself.

## Safe by design

The page only reads public accounts. It never asks for your wallet, an address of yours, a signature or a key. It uses free public Solana nodes in turn, or your own node if you paste one. A shared link with `?mint=` lets anyone repeat exactly the same check.

## For shops and finance teams

The same habit protects a business: before you accept a token, list it in a treasury or pay a supplier in it, read its powers. Can someone freeze our balance? Is there a fee on every transfer that breaks our accounting? Can a delegate move funds out? Five minutes on the page answers these from the chain, not from a marketing page.
