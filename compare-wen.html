    How the procedure works

    1. Your browser sends one read-only request, getAccountInfo, for the WEN mint and one for the token you chose, to a free public Solana node (PublicNode, dRPC or the Solana Foundation node, whichever answers first) or to your own. Nothing is signed, no wallet is connected; only the two mint addresses leave your browser.
    2. The answer comes back decoded by the RPC node (jsonParsed): program, supply, authorities and Token-2022 extensions.
    3. Each power is turned into plain words: mint and freeze authority, transfer fee, permanent delegate, transfer hook and who can change it, accounts frozen by default, non-transferable, close and pause authority.
    4. The page then reads the two programs built around WEN that the almanac records, the WNS program (wns1g…) and its royalty transfer-hook program (wen_royalty_distribution, diste3n…), and says whether they are live, executable code on mainnet. The poem NFT's own mint address is not yet in the almanac record, so the page checks the programs its hook relies on.
    5. It then asks the public IQ.wiki encyclopedia whether a wiki page exists: for WEN (iq.wiki/wiki/wen, listed in the almanac's sources) and for the compared token, by its name and address. A page found means public documentation exists; no page says nothing about the token itself.
    6. The two columns are lined up; rows that differ are tinted, keys that can still act are marked, and every row has a What it means button with a plain explanation and the picture story about it. Below, 20 facts from the almanac record appear next to the compared token. The link with ?mint= lets anyone repeat the same check.

    Read it fairly

    A key that can act is not good or bad by itself. Stablecoins and regulated assets often must keep a freeze authority or a permanent delegate by law; a meme coin usually gives these keys up. The page compares powers, it does not judge projects. Preset addresses are common community references: the page always reads the chain, so check that the address is the one you mean. The mint itself is only part of the picture; programs behind a transfer hook need their own review.
