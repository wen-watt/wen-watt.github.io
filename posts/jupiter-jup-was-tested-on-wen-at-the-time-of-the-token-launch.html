The Dress Rehearsal: Why Jupiter Tested Its Launchpad on WEN

July 10, 2025

Historical post This post records events as they were reported at the time. Facts are checked against sources; nothing here is a forecast.

Before Jupiter airdropped its own token to the whole Solana community, it needed to know whether its launchpad, its claim system and the network itself would survive a million people arriving at once. The answer was WEN. This post records the dress rehearsal — and checks a long list of "twenty technical benefits" that grew around it, keeping only what can be shown.

Why rehearse

Software can be tested in a lab, but a public launch is different: real wallets, real bots, real congestion. Jupiter chose to run its first launchpad event with a community coin before the event that mattered most — its own token. If something broke, it would break on a meme, with lessons learned before $JUP.

The rehearsal, day by day

  • January 25, 2024 — Meow announces "Immortalizing WEN Culture"; the WNS 0.0 proposal is posted.
  • January 26, 2024 — WEN launches as the first token on the LFG Launchpad; more than a million wallets can claim. The network stays up — see the stress test.
  • January 29, 2024 — the claim window closes; more than 271 billion unclaimed WEN, over 27% of supply, are burned (Decrypt).
  • January 31, 2024 — the $JUP airdrop follows, with the lessons applied.

One correction to the original: the poem was split into one trillion tokens, not a billion.

What the rehearsal taught

  • The chain held under a peak of thousands of transactions per second.
  • Bots were the weak spot. Jupiter's co-founder said so publicly the same day — and fair-launch defences became a priority.
  • Claim windows work. A fixed window plus a burn of the remainder left no ambiguous leftover supply.
  • Standards can ride along. WNS was introduced with the launch, so the experiment produced a tool, not only a token.
