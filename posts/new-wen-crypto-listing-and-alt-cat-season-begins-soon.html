First, the promise

"$1,000 to $100,000" is the oldest line in the market. It works because we only hear from the few who won; the many who lost are silent. That is survivorship bias, and any post that sells it should be read as an advertisement, not information. The cat-season mood of the time is real culture — read Cat Season — but culture is not a promise of returns.

What a memecoin is, technically

On Solana, a memecoin is not special code. It is a token mint created by the standard token program — the same program that runs stablecoins and governance tokens. A mint has a few properties that matter far more than any meme:

  • Supply — how many tokens exist, and whether more can ever be created.
  • Mint authority — who, if anyone, can print new tokens.
  • Freeze authority — who, if anyone, can freeze accounts.
  • Distribution — how tokens first reached people: a sale, a team allocation, or an airdrop.
  • Extensions — with Token-2022, a mint can carry rules such as transfer fees or metadata; WNS builds NFTs with enforced royalties on exactly these extensions.

WEN's distinguishing technical fact is its launch: a public airdrop to more than a million wallets that doubled as a stress test of Solana — and its tie to the open Wen New Standard.

Five things to verify

  1. The mint address — names and tickers are copied every day; the address is unique.
  2. The authorities — can someone print more or freeze your tokens?
  3. The distribution — who received the supply at launch?
  4. The sources — are claims backed by an official site or only by screenshots?
  5. The pitch — any promise of multiplied money is a warning sign by itself.

Each of these can be checked on a public explorer — for WEN, start with the Solana Explorer — and in the trust chain.
