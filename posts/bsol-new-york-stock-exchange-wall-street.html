BSOL on the NYSE: Solana Reaches Wall Street, and What It Means for Wen

October 30, 2025

At the end of October 2025, Solana walked onto Wall Street. On October 28, Bitwise listed the Bitwise Solana Staking ETF on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker BSOL — a regulated fund that holds SOL and stakes it. For a network that many people still knew only through meme coins, it was a moment of recognition. This post explains what BSOL is, what it is not, and what it does — and does not — mean for the Wen cat.

What BSOL is

  • Name and ticker: Bitwise Solana Staking ETF, BSOL.
  • Where: New York Stock Exchange, first trading day October 28, 2025.
  • What it holds: SOL, Solana's native token — directly, not through futures.
  • What makes it different: the fund stakes its SOL, so it earns network staking rewards, and it was among the first U.S. spot crypto funds built around staking from day one.
  • Fees: a 0.20% management fee, waived for the first three months after launch.

In plain words: BSOL lets someone with an ordinary brokerage account get exposure to SOL without running a wallet or a validator. It does not hold Wen, Bonk, Jupiter or any other Solana token.

The first days, in checked numbers

  • BSOL drew $69.5 million of inflows on its first day, according to Decrypt's report on the debut.
  • Grayscale's Solana fund, GSOL, was approved and set to start trading the following day, so the U.S. market went from zero to two Solana products within a week.
  • Bitwise's chief investment officer Matt Hougan summed up the mood in one line: "I have a feeling the Bitwise Solana Staking ETF, BSOL, is gonna be huge."

The original post also cited big projections — billions of dollars in the first year, multi-fold gains for ecosystem tokens, double-digit volume jumps. We have removed them. They were forecasts, not facts, and some were never sourced unverified.

Why staking matters

Solana is secured by proof of stake: validators lock up SOL as a guarantee of good behaviour, and the network rewards them for producing and checking blocks. When a large fund stakes its holdings, more SOL is working to secure the chain. Bitwise said it stakes the fund's SOL through its own staking operation; the original post named Helius as the infrastructure provider reported.

Staking rewards on Solana have historically been around 6–7% a year, but they vary with network conditions and are not guaranteed. A fund's investors receive what is left after fees and the fund's own policies, so any single "yield" figure is a snapshot, not a promise.

What it means for Solana

For years, Solana's reputation swung between "fastest chain" and "meme casino". A staking ETF on the NYSE adds a third description: an asset that regulated institutions are willing to package for ordinary investors. Within the same week, Western Union announced a stablecoin on Solana — our post on Western Union and USDPT covers it — and the following year brought tokenization pilots in South Korea, described in our Korea post. Together they tell the story we track in How SOL earned global recognition.

Solana's official account marked the moment too: Solana on X.

And for Wen?

Here we have to be honest, because hype is easy. BSOL does not buy WEN. No ETF money flows into a meme coin automatically. What a Solana ETF does change is the neighbourhood: more people learn what Solana is, more wallets and apps grow up around it, and the culture that lives on the chain — the Wen cat, the Bonk dog, the Jupiter community — gets a bigger audience. Whether that audience cares about a cat born from a poem is up to the community, not to Wall Street opinion.
