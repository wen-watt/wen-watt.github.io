Where the word "wen" comes from

In crypto slang, "wen" is a playful misspelling of "when" — as in the endless questions "wen moon?" and "wen lambo?". It captures the impatience of a market that always wants the future now. The original post added a lovely bit of wordplay from Chinese, where similar-sounding syllables mean 问 (wèn, "to ask"), 文 (wén, "writing, culture") and 温 (wēn, "warm"). Ask, culture, warmth — not a bad motto for a community coin wordplay.

Cats themselves have a far older history with people than any coin: from the guardians of Egyptian granaries (see Wen and the cats of ancient Egypt) to the cats that pull the goddess Freyja's chariot in Norse myth.

Wen's place in the story

Wen sits at a crossroads of three eras. From the NFT era it took its form: a poem minted as an NFT and fractionalized into one trillion tokens. From the memecoin era it took its culture: a cat, jokes, art, a community. And from the infrastructure era it took its lasting contribution: the Wen New Standard on Token-2022 and a public stress test that prepared Solana for much larger launches.

Hopes, clearly marked

The original described 2025 onward as "the Wen era" of an "energy coin" — a third model beside stablecoins and memecoins opinion. It is a community dream, and we keep it as one. We removed what was not history: price levels, a hypothetical comparison with another coin's record, claims of exchange listings, and partnerships we could not confirm (the trust chain lists them as unverified).

What the eras teach

  1. Every era leaves tools behind. Bitcoin left a secure ledger, Ethereum smart contracts, DeFi open finance — and Wen left an open NFT standard.
  2. Hype ends, infrastructure stays. ICO tokens vanished; the standards and rails built in those years are still in use.
  3. History is written afterwards. That is why this almanac records events with sources and keeps forecasts out.
