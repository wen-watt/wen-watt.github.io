Why a quiz

The Wen almanac has grown into dozens of posts, nine key pages and a whole workshop, Wen Open Lab. That is a lot to read. A quiz turns it around: instead of asking you to read everything first, it asks you a question, lets you guess, and then hands you exactly the piece of knowledge you were missing. Wrong answers are not punished; they are the point. Each one comes with a knowledge card — "here is how it really is" — and a link to go deeper.

The format borrows the energy of quick logo-guessing games: short rounds, instant feedback, a score you want to beat. But the content is entirely ours, built from the almanac and checked against the trust chain.

How it works

  • One round, ten questions. No levels to unlock and nowhere to get stuck. Each round mixes three fun questions, three basics, three technical ones and one about safety or the wider world.
  • Four options, one right answer. Click, tap or press 1–4. The options are shuffled every time.
  • Points for knowing, a bonus for speed. A right answer earns 100 points plus a speed bonus of up to 50 that shrinks by 5 every second. The maximum is 1,500.
  • A card for every miss. Your wrong pick turns red, the right one green, and a short explanation appears with a "Read more" link.
  • A rank at the end. From Newborn kitten through Curious kitten, Wen explorer and Wen scholar to Wen legend, plus a list of the questions worth a second look — and a button to play again with a fresh mix.

What the quiz teaches about Wen's usefulness

Play a few rounds and a picture forms — not of a price, but of what Wen actually brought to Solana:

  1. A fair start. A token born from a poem and airdropped to more than a million wallets, without a private sale. That is a lesson in distribution that every new project can study.
  2. An open standard. The Wen New Standard showed how to build lightweight NFTs on Token-2022 with royalties enforced by the token itself. The code is open, and the Lab turns it into receipts, royalties and invoices anyone can test.
  3. A public stress test. The WEN launch pushed Solana and its wallets to their limits in January 2024 — and the lessons fed straight into the much larger $JUP airdrop.
  4. A culture that travels. A cat everyone recognises, free community art, friendships with Bonk and Jupiter, plush toys for charity, football in Buenos Aires — culture is how strangers first meet a technology.
  5. Safety habits. One contract address, checked every time; no seed phrase on any website; no installing packages that were pulled as malicious. The safety questions are the ones we hope you never get wrong.

Facts only, no tracking

Two promises hold the quiz together. First, every answer is built only on claims marked Verified, Primary source or Reported in the trust chain; opinions and unconfirmed rumours stay out, however popular. Second, nothing is collected. There is no account, no cookie and no analytics; the only thing remembered is your best score, stored in your own browser. Our editorial and data policy explains the same rules for the whole site.

What comes next

The almanac keeps growing, and the quiz grows with it: every rewritten post adds its checked facts to the base, and every new fact also gets its own photo in the Wen Image Stories. It started with 100 questions; today it holds 250, including Meow's return on Planetary Call #60, Western Union's stablecoin on Solana and Bitcoin's proof of work. The rules stay the same: checked facts only, a card for every answer, a link for every card.

How to take part

  • Play and share your rank. A Wen legend screenshot says more than any advert.
  • Suggest a question. Send it to the community on X with its source; if it passes the trust chain, it goes in.
  • Read the cards. Every one leads to a page; the almanac is the long version of every answer.
  • Build something. If the tech questions made you curious, Wen Open Lab has free models waiting.
