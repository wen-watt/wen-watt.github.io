The SEC and the 19b-4 filings

Until September 2025, every new crypto exchange-traded fund needed two filings: an S-1 describing the fund, and a 19b-4 asking the SEC to change an exchange's rules so it could list that fund. In mid-September 2025 the SEC approved generic listing standards for commodity-based products, including crypto. On September 29, 2025, it asked issuers of proposed ETFs for SOL, XRP, LTC, ADA and DOGE to withdraw their 19b-4 filings, which were no longer needed — only the S-1 remained (FXStreet report).

In plain words: crypto funds stopped being handled as exceptions and started being handled by a standard procedure. Analysts at the time said approvals could come quickly, while noting that no timeline was guaranteed.

The tax note: what it really said

The headline that went around — "U.S. Treasury exempts Bitcoin and crypto from the 15% corporate minimum tax" — was louder than the rule. What happened: on September 30, 2025 the IRS released Notice 2025-49, interim guidance for the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT), a 15% minimum tax on very large companies' book income. It lets these companies elect to leave out unrealized gains and losses on certain assets measured at fair value — which can include digital assets — when computing that income (Vedder Price summary).

  • It concerns very large corporations, not individuals.
  • It removes tax on paper gains under this one regime; it is not a general exemption for crypto.
  • It is elective and interim, pending final regulations.

What happened next

The standard procedure worked as described: within weeks, Solana funds began trading — in Hong Kong and on the New York Stock Exchange in late October 2025. The full story is in BSOL on Wall Street.

Five on-chain numbers, explained

NumberWhat it measuresWhat it does not tell you
HoldersWallets with a non-zero balance of a tokenHow many people: one person can have many wallets, and an airdrop creates holders who never chose to hold
Active walletsWallets that sent or received the token in a periodWhether the activity is people or bots
Transactions per second (TPS)How many transactions the network processesOn Solana, most transactions are validator votes; "user" TPS is a much smaller, different number
VolumeThe value of a token traded in a periodWhether trades are real: the same coins can be passed back and forth
ConcentrationHow much of the supply sits in the largest walletsWho owns them: many are exchanges, pools or programs, not individuals

Moving averages and oscillators such as RSI, quoted in the original, are calculations on past prices. They describe what already happened; they do not know what will happen next. That is why this almanac does not publish them.

How these numbers mislead

  • Short windows exaggerate. "+25% in a week" can be a few thousand wallets reacting to one post.
  • Bots look like people. Automated trading and airdrop farming inflate activity counts.
  • Holders are not believers. WEN reached more than a million wallets through an airdrop; many never touched it again.
  • "Whale accumulation" is a guess. A big wallet buying may be an exchange moving customer funds.
