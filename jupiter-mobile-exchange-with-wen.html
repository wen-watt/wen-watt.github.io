Atomic transaction: in crypto, an atomic transaction is a group of operations handled as one unit. Either every operation in the group succeeds, or none of them runs. This keeps data consistent and prevents problems such as double-spending or half-finished transactions, especially in decentralized systems.

In more detail: Data consistency: they are key to keeping data intact, above all in complex transactions with several parties or blockchains. Indivisibility: an atomic transaction cannot be split; it happens fully or not at all. No intermediaries: atomic transactions can remove the need for trusted third parties such as exchanges, enabling trustless peer-to-peer trading. Smart contracts: they usually rely on smart contracts to enforce the "all or nothing" rule. Decentralized systems: atomic transactions are essential where many parties may touch the same data at once, as on blockchains. Example — atomic swaps: a common case is an atomic swap, where two parties trade coins across different blockchains. The swap only goes through if both sides deposit their assets into a smart contract; if either fails to do so in time, everything is reversed. Two-phase commit: some systems use a two-phase commit, preparing the operations first and then either committing or rolling them back.
